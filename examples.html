- Top 3 -

Winning List: Conan Jennings (9-0-0)

The Ultramarines have pivoted away from the traditional "Calgar Castle" toward a high-mobility, high-pressure list that exploits the Gladius Task Force or the new Blade of Ultramar detachment.

Why it's powerful: It has the best "Action Economy" in the game. With the ability to Advance and Charge or Fall Back and Shoot, they are never pinned down.

Unit Roles:

Leader: Marneus Calgar (Terminator Armor). He generates extra CP and provides incredible melee punch to a Terminator brick.

Objective Holders: Heavy Intercessors. T6 and 3 wounds make them a nightmare to shift from the home objective.

Trade Units: Assault Intercessors with Jump Packs. Fast, cheap, and capable of killing a unit twice their cost before dying.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancer. The most efficient long-range AT in the game due to its built-in rerolls.

Secondary Scorers: Inceptors. Their 3" deep strike allows them to score "Behind Enemy Lines" or "Deploy Teleport Homers" anywhere on the board.

Punchback: Brutalis Dreadnought. If an enemy charges your front line, this is the unit that crashes in next turn to wipe them out.

Anvil: Terminator Squad (10-man). Combined with a Librarian for Sustained Hits, they sit in the center and simply refuse to die.

Transport: Impulsor. Used to ferry small squads of Hellblasters or Company Heroes to mid-board safely.

Winning List: Brian Daugherty (8-1-0)

This list focuses on the Renegade Raiders detachment, which grants extra AP and movement speed when targeting enemies on objectives.

Why it's powerful: It is arguably the fastest army in the game. It uses "Scout" moves and "Dark Pacts" to ensure that whoever they hit stays dead.

Unit Roles:

Leader: Fabius Bile. He buffs a unit of Chosen to T5, making them significantly harder to kill in the mid-board.

Objective Holders: Cultist Mob. Cheap, disposable, and they "Sticky" objectives so the rest of the army can move forward.

Trade Units: Warp Talons. With their ability to go back into Strategic Reserves after killing a unit, they "trade" and then hide.

Anti-Tank: Chaos Vindicator. The Demolisher Cannon is the ultimate "point and delete" tool for enemy armor.

Secondary Scorers: Nurglings (Allied). Still the gold standard for cheap infiltrators that screen and score "Cleanse."

Punchback: Chaos Chosen. They can Advance, Shoot, and Charge, making them the perfect reactionary unit.

Anvil: Land Raider. Not just a transport, but a T12 brick that demands the opponent's entire shooting phase to kill.

Transport: Rhino. Essential for protecting the Chosen and Legionaries as they race toward the center.

Winning List: Jordan Nach (Top 3 Major Placer)

Despite point increases to C'tan, the Necrons remain dominant because their "stat checks" (High Toughness/Feel No Pain) are too much for many armies to handle.

Why it's powerful: Reanimation Protocols. Even if you don't kill a unit entirely, it comes back. They play the "Primary Score" game better than anyone else.

Unit Roles:

Leader: Technomancer. Usually attached to Wraiths to give them a 5+ Feel No Pain, making them arguably the tankiest unit in the meta.

Objective Holders: Immortals (with Plasmancer). They provide high-volume fire and are surprisingly durable for Battleline.

Trade Units: Skorpekh Destroyers. They go in, kill an elite squad, and if they die, it’s fine—they’ve done their job.

Anti-Tank: Canoptek Doomstalker. Since the 2026 update, these have become staple long-range fire support for the Awakened Dynasty.

Secondary Scorers: Deathmarks. Snipers that can Deep Strike into corners to score "Investigate Signals."

Punchback: C'tan Shard of the Nightbringer. The ultimate deterrent. If you move close to it, it will charge and destroy anything in the game.

Anvil: Canoptek Wraiths. With 4 wounds, a 4++ Invuln, and a 5+++ FNP, they are the definition of an immovable object.

Transport: Night Scythe / Monolith. While rarely used as a "bus," the Monolith’s ability to teleport units across the board serves as the ultimate tactical transport.

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Captain in Terminator Armor
Equipped with the Fire Discipline enhancement, he is a force multiplier. When his unit is in the Devastator Doctrine, they gain Sustained Hits 1 and Critical Hits on a 5+. This turns a standard Terminator squad into a high-volume fire platform that can shred light tanks and heavy infantry alike.

Anvil: Aggressor Squad (with Apothecary Biologis)
This is your "Mid-board Bully." The Biologis grants the unit Lethal Hits. In the Gladius detachment, you use the Aggressive Surge stratagem to ensure they reach the center. With T6 and a high volume of shots, they are incredibly difficult to shift and punish anyone who tries to charge them.

Objective Holders: Heavy Intercessors
While standard Intercessors provide Sticky Objectives, the Heavy Intercessors are your "Hardened Point." With T6 and +1 to their armor saves against Damage 1 weapons, they sit on the most contested objective and dare the enemy to waste high-quality anti-tank shots on them.

Trade Units: Jump Pack Intercessors
These are your "Harassers." With a 12" move and the Hammer of Wrath ability (dealing mortal wounds on the charge), they are perfect for diving onto an enemy's home objective to kill a small unit of Cultists or Grots. You trade them to disrupt the opponent's "Primary" scoring.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancer
The Lancer is the most reliable sniper in the Astartes armory. Its Aquila Optics allow for a hit, wound, and damage reroll every time it shoots. It stays in the back corners, drawing lines of fire to the most dangerous enemy vehicles.

Secondary Scorers: Scouts
In 2026, Scouts are mandatory. Their Guerrilla Tactics allow them to go into Strategic Reserves at the end of the enemy turn. They spend the game hopping around the board corners to score "Investigate Signals" or "Deploy Teleport Homers."

Punchback: Hellblasters (with Lieutenant)
This unit lives inside the Repulsor. The Lieutenant grants them Lethal Hits and Fall Back and Shoot. If an enemy charges them, they shoot. If they die, their For the Chapter! rule lets them shoot one last time on a 3+. They are your "Insurance Policy"—if the enemy moves into the open, the Hellblasters erase them.

Transport: Repulsor
The Repulsor is more than a taxi; it is a mobile bunker. Its Emergency Embarkation ability allows a nearby unit (like the Hellblasters) to hop back inside if they are charged, effectively wasting the opponent's turn and keeping your high-value plasma guns safe.

Why this list wins:
It wins through Flexibility. Because you have access to the Tactical Doctrine (Advance and Shoot) and the Assault Doctrine (Advance and Charge), you are never "out of range." You have the anti-tank to kill Knights, the volume of fire to kill hordes, and the mobility to play the mission. It is the "Swiss Army Knife" of 40k.

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: High Marshal Helbrecht
Helbrecht is a melee monster. He grants his unit +1 Strength and +1 Critical Hit range (meaning they trigger Lethal or Sustained Hits on a 5+). When leading Sword Brethren, he makes them capable of wounding T12 vehicles on a 4+ or 5+, which is terrifying for a pure infantry unit.

Leader: Chaplain Grimaldus
Grimaldus is the "Durability Engine." His Cenobyte Servitors provide three different auras; typically, you choose the 5+ Feel No Pain or the "Advance and Charge" prayer. Putting him in a 20-man Crusader squad creates a 40-wound block that most opponents simply cannot kill in five turns.

Anvil: Primaris Crusader Squad (20-man)
This is the heart of the Templars. By mixing Initiates and Neophytes, you create a massive footprint that denies enemy movement. Under the Uphold the Honour vow, this entire block has a 6+ Feel No Pain and a 5+ Invulnerable save, making them the most stubborn "Anvil" in the Space Marine codex.

Objective Holders: Intercessors
Standard "Sticky Objective" duty. They tap the home point and then move to the flanks to prevent enemy "Action Monkeys" (like Nurglings or Mandrakes) from landing in your backline.

Trade Units: Impulsors (with Multi-meltas)
Templar Impulsors are unique because they can take a Multi-melta. In the 2026 meta, these are "Active Traders." You drive them aggressively into the mid-board to drop off small squads of Sword Brethren, then use the tank itself to block charge lanes or take potshots at enemy light armor.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancers
Because the rest of the army is so melee-focused, you need the Lancers to handle "Flyers" or heavy tanks that you can't easily reach. They provide the S12 "anchor fire" that forces your opponent to keep their tanks hidden.

Secondary Scorers: Scouts
With Infiltrate and the ability to go back into reserves, Scouts handle your "Cleanse" and "Deploy Teleport Homers" actions. They are the only units in your army allowed to not be in combat.

Punchback: The Emperor’s Champion
The Champion is a heat-seeing missile for enemy characters. With Precision and S8 high-AP attacks, he can jump out of a Land Raider and "one-shot" an enemy Captain or Librarian. He is your insurance policy against powerful enemy buff-characters.

Transport: Land Raider Crusader
The "Brick" that carries Helbrecht and the 10-man Sword Brethren squad. With Assault Ramp, it allows the Templars to move 12", disembark, and charge, giving your most lethal melee unit a massive threat range that ignores most enemy screens.

Why this list wins:
It wins through Relentless Pressure. The opponent has to deal with two Gladiator Lancers in the back, a 20-man Grimaldus-led squad in the center, and a Land Raider full of Helbrecht’s elites screaming down one flank. The Black Templars don't play a "finesse" game—they overwhelm the opponent’s ability to target-prioritize until the enemy is simply out of models.

Secondary Scorers: 5x Infiltrators (Helix Gauntlet & Omni-scrambler)

Punchback: The Sanguinor

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Commander Dante
Dante is your "Force Multiplier." He grants his unit +1 to Hit and, more importantly, ensures they are never affected by negative modifiers to their Move, Advance, or Charge rolls. In 2026, Dante ensures the Sanguinary Guard hit their target like a gold-plated sledgehammer.

Leader: Lemartes
Lemartes is the "Anchor of Madness." He grants his Death Company unit a 6+ Feel No Pain (improving their durability) and reduces the Damage characteristic of incoming attacks by 1. He makes the glass-cannon Death Company surprisingly difficult to remove before they reach melee.

Anvil: Death Company (Jump Packs)
This is your "Turn One Missile." With a 12" move and the ability to re-roll charges, they are the most feared melee unit in the game. In the Liberator Assault Group, they hit at S10 on the charge with their Power Fists, capable of "one-shotting" a Knight or a Greater Daemon.

Objective Holders: Intercessors & Assault Intercessors
Intercessors: Use their Sticky Objectives to claim the backfield and then move up to provide fire support.
Assault Intercessors: They sit in the Impulsor. Their job is to hold the mid-board; they reroll Wounds when attacking enemies on objectives, making them "Objective Clearers" in the early game.

Trade Units: Jump Pack Intercessors
These are your "Hammer of Wrath" traders. They zip across the board to harass small enemy units or tie up tanks. You trade their 80-point cost to ensure your opponent has to spend a turn shooting them instead of Dante or Lemartes.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancer & Baal Predator
Gladiator Lancer: Provides the necessary S12+ long-range support to crack armor before the melee units arrive.
Baal Predator: The "Overwatch King." Its Flamestorm Cannon is an 18" auto-hitting nightmare. It drives straight into the center to create a "No-Man's Land" where enemy infantry fear to tread.

Secondary Scorers: Scouts & Infiltrators
Scouts: Essential for their Guerrilla Tactics, allowing them to go into reserves and reappear to score secondaries.
Infiltrators: Their 12" "No-Deep Strike" bubble is vital for protecting your backline from Grey Knights or Aeldari teleporting into your deployment zone.

Punchback: The Sanguinor
The Sanguinor is the ultimate "Gotcha" unit. He doesn't start on the board; he uses Miraculous Intervention to drop into combat the moment an enemy charges one of your units. He strikes first with a high volume of high-AP attacks, often killing the enemy charger before they even get to swing at your troops.

Why this list wins:
The Blood Angels win through Violence and Momentum. By combining the Sanguinary Guard's elite precision with the Death Company's raw strength, you create a "Dual Threat" that most armies cannot screen against. You use the Baal Predator to flush enemies out of cover, then use your Jump Pack units to finish them off in the Assault phase.

Trade Units: 3x Ravenwing Black Knights (Corvus Hammers)

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancer (Lancer Laser Destroyer)

Secondary Scorers: 5x Scouts (with Sniper Rifle & Missile Launcher)

Punchback: 5x Deathwing Knights (led by Belial)

Transport: Repulsor Executioner (Heavy Laser Destroyer)

Transport: Razorback (Twin Lascannon)

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Lion El'Jonson
The Primarch is your "un-killable" center. His All-slayer profile deletes monsters and tanks, but his Aura of the Lion is what wins games—granting nearby units a 4+ Feel No Pain against Mortal Wounds. In 2026, he is the ultimate deterrent; you don't charge the Lion, you run away from him.

Leader: Azrael
Azrael is a "CP Battery." He stays in the back or mid-board with his Company Heroes, generating an extra Command Point every turn. He also grants his unit a 4+ Invulnerable save, making the Company Heroes one of the most points-efficient objective holders in the game.

Anvil: Deathwing Knights
With T5, 4 Wounds, and a -1 damage modifier, Deathwing Knights are the premier heavy infantry of the Imperium. They are the units you place on your "Vowed Objective." With full rerolls to wound from the detachment, their Maces of Absolution will crush even the toughest Chaos Terminators or Tyranid monsters.

Objective Holders: Intercessors & Company Heroes
Intercessors: Used for Sticky Objectives. They tap the home point and then move to screen the Gladiator Lancer.
Company Heroes: Their Bodyguard rule makes Azrael nearly impossible to snipe, while their diverse weapon profiles allow them to fend off light infantry probes.

Trade Units: Ravenwing Black Knights
These are your "Anti-Elite" specialists. With their Anti-Vehicle/Monster 4+ on their Corvus Hammers, they can charge a unit significantly more expensive than themselves and deal massive damage before being wiped out. They use their high movement to force the opponent to react.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancer & Repulsor Executioner
Dark Angels play a "clean" game. The Lancer provides consistent long-range removal, while the Repulsor Executioner acts as both a heavy tank and a transport for the Lion or a small squad of infantry, providing a massive S16 threat to the board.

Secondary Scorers: Scouts
Scouts are the utility kings. With Infiltrate and the ability to go into Strategic Reserves at the end of the turn, they score "Signals" and "Cleanse" with ease. Their sniper rifles also provide a "Precision" threat to pesky enemy buff-characters.

Punchback: Deathwing Knights (with Belial)
Belial grants his unit Precision on critical hits. By keeping this unit in Deep Strike, you can drop them 9" away from an enemy leader. If the enemy moves onto your Vowed Objective, these Knights charge in with rerolls and eliminate the enemy character with surgical precision.

Transport: Repulsor Executioner & Razorback
The Razorback provides fire support for the Intercessors, while the Executioner ensures your heavy-hitters get to the center of the board without being picked off by the "trade pieces" of other factions.

Why this list wins:
The Dark Angels win through Focus. By picking one objective to be "Vowed," you ensure that the math is always in your favor on the most important part of the board. Between the Lion's defensive auras and the Deathwing's inherent toughness, you simply play a more efficient version of the game than your opponent.

Secondary Scorers: 5x Purgation Squad (4x Incinerators)

Punchback: 5x Paladins (3x Psycannons, led by Librarian)

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Kaldor Draigo
Draigo is your "Reliable Charge" button. His One-man Army rule gives his unit +3 to charge rolls when arriving from Deep Strike. In a meta where 9" charges are risky, Draigo makes a 6" charge almost guaranteed, allowing his Terminators to delete a key enemy unit the moment they arrive.

Anvil: Brotherhood Terminators
These are the most annoying units to kill in the game. With the Narthecium, they return one dead model to the unit every single Command Phase. If your opponent doesn't wipe the whole squad, they just keep coming back. They use Lethal Hits on the charge to punch up into high-toughness targets.

Objective Holders: Strike Squads
Strike Squads are essential because of Sanctifying Ritual. This gives their objectives Sticky Objectives, allowing the Grey Knights to capture a point and immediately teleport away to a different part of the board while still scoring the points.

Trade Units: Interceptor Squads
With a 12" move and the ability to move again after shooting, Interceptors are your "harassment" units. You use them to jump over screens, kill a small support unit like a GSC Nexos or an Aeldari Warlock, and then hide behind a wall before the enemy can swing back.

Anti-Tank: Nemesis Dreadknights
In the 2026 update, Dreadknights have S12 melee weapons, making them the primary anti-tank tool. The Grand Master version with the Sigil of Exigence is particularly dangerous; once per game, when he is targeted by a ranged attack, he can immediately teleport away, wasting the opponent's shots.

Secondary Scorers: Purgation Squad
Equipped with Incinerators (flamers), these units use Astral Aim to shoot at targets they can't even see (Indirect Fire). They are perfect for clearing small units hiding in ruins to score "Cleanse" or "Investigate Signals" from total safety.

Punchback: Paladins & Librarian
Paladins hit on 2+ and are T6, making them much sturdier than regular Terminators. The Librarian provides Vortex of Doom, a psychic power that deals mortal wounds without needing a hit roll. They are your "Mid-board Bully" unit that stays in the center and dares the enemy to approach.

Transport: (Teleport Assault)
The Grey Knights don't use Rhinos or Land Raiders because the entire army can fly. The Teleport Assault rule allows you to pick up up to 3 units every turn and place them in Deep Strike. This "virtual transport" makes the Grey Knights the most mobile army in Warhammer 40k.

Why this list wins:
It wins through Positioning. You never have to walk across the board and get shot. You start in cover, teleport to where the enemy is weak, strike, and then use Mists of Deimos to teleport away when the enemy tries to charge you. You are playing a game of "cat and mouse" where you are always the cat.

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Logan Grimnar on Stormrider
Logan is the "Alpha." Once per game, his High King of Fenris ability allows the entire army to reroll successful charges and—crucially—grants everyone extra melee attacks. In the 2026 meta, Logan’s "Waaagh-style" turn is usually when the game is decided.

Anvil: Thunderwolf Cavalry (The Deathstar)
With T6, 4 Wounds, and a 4+ Invulnerable Save from their Storm Shields, these are the most durable cavalry in the game. When led by a Wolf Lord and a Battle Leader, they gain Lethal Hits and Devastating Wounds. In the Stormlance detachment, they can move 10", Advance, and still Charge, giving them a massive 17-22" threat range.

Objective Holders: Intercessors
These are your "Sticky Objective" units. Their Objective Secured rule allows them to tap a point and then move forward to screen for the cavalry, ensuring your primary score stays high even if the unit is eventually wiped out.

Trade Units: Fenrisian Wolves
At a tiny point cost, these are your "Screen-Clearers." You throw them at the enemy's front line to absorb Overwatch fire or to block an enemy scout move. They are intended to die to keep your Thunderwolves healthy for the real fight.

Anti-Tank: Gladiator Lancers
Space Wolves need reliable long-range support to crack heavy T12 targets. The Lancer’s built-in rerolls for hits, wounds, and damage make it the most consistent anti-tank platform in the Astartes arsenal, allowing your melee units to focus on clearing objectives.

Secondary Scorers: Wulfen
Wulfen are the ultimate "distraction carnifex." With their 4+ Invulnerable save and the ability to move 8", they zip into corners for "Deploy Teleport Homers." If they are ignored, they charge and kill elite infantry; if they are shot, they have "Fight on Death," ensuring the opponent pays a price.

Punchback: Wolf Guard Terminators
Kept in Teleport Strike (Deep Strike), these are your "Reactionary Hammer." When an enemy commits their heavy-hitter to the center objective, the Terminators drop in. With Storm Shields and Power Fists, they can weather the storm and punch back with S8 high-damage swings.

Why this list wins:
It wins through Aggression and Durability. The Stormlance detachment allows you to dictate the engagement. By Turn 2, you are almost guaranteed to be in the enemy's deployment zone. The opponent is forced to choose between shooting at the Thunderwolves (who have a 4++ save) or the Gladiator Lancers (who are deleting their tanks). Most opponents simply lack the volume of fire to stop both.

2. Role Breakdown

Leader: Morvenn Vahl
Vahl is the undisputed queen of the Sisters' offensive. Her Abbeys of the Golden Light rule allows her and her Paragon Warsuits to reroll all Hit and Wound rolls. In the 2026 meta, she is a heat-seeking missile that can delete a Greater Daemon or a Land Raider in a single turn.

Objective Holders: Battle Sister Squads
These squads are the lifeblood of the army. Their Sacred Grounds rule generates a Miracle Die every turn they hold an objective. By 2026 standards, they are "sticky," using the Litanies of Faith to stay relevant even as they take casualties.

Trade Units: Seraphim
With their Burning Descent ability, they Deep Strike, fire their hand flamers (benefiting from the detachment's +1 to Wound for acts of faith), and then move 6" again. You trade them to clear an enemy's light infantry off a backfield point, forcing the opponent to redirect fire away from your tanks.

Punchback: Zephyrim (with Saint Celestine)
